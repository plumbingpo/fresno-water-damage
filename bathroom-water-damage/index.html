Bathroom Water Damage in Fresno

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. It collects in the subfloor and the wall behind the tile, out of sight. In Fresno, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Signs to Look For

  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • Floor tiles that sound hollow, rock underfoot, or have come loose near the tub
  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • Grout lines that stay dark and damp long after the last shower
  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
  • The caulk line at the shower or tub has split, pulled away, or turned black
Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Slow Vanity Drips

Months can pass with a supply valve or drain joint dripping, hidden by whatever is stored in the vanity. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

The Room Below

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

Rotted Floor at the Toilet

Each flush leaks a small amount once the wax ring fails. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.

Poor Ventilation

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First days

Water Gets Past

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. The repair now includes carpentry and new tile, not only drying.

What We Do

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

Loose tile, rotted subfloor and soft wall board come out. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Cavities

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Nothing is sealed back up until we have treated with antimicrobial and the readings show dry.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.
